Institution matrix

Ten institutions organize recurring social needs

An institution is not one building or organization. It is an enduring arrangement of values, norms, statuses, roles, and practices. These examples describe common patterns, not rules that every society or person must follow.

Economic comparison

Economic systems are ideal types, not sealed boxes

The central correction: Modern economies are hybrids. Compare ownership, coordination, public provision, regulation, and power separately instead of assigning an entire country one pure label.

Application

Describe a society without forcing it into one box

  1. Identify who owns major productive resources.
  2. Explain when markets, government planning, household labor, or custom allocates goods.
  3. Identify how leaders are selected and removed.
  4. Examine who can participate and what constrains authority.
  5. Ask who controls information, technology, money, and organized force in practice.
  6. Name one cultural lag created when technology changes before law or norms catch up.
Eleanor-family prompt: Compare Grace’s 1988 workplace computer with an algorithmically managed workplace today. What became faster? What new risks appeared? Which institution should respond, and whose voice should shape the response?
