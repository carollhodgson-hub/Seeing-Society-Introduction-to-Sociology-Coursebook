Groups, Deviance, Social Control, and Social Interaction

🌟 Why This Section Matters

Humans are social beings. We belong to groups, compare ourselves to others, follow norms, break norms, perform roles, and respond to social pressure. This mini-chapter helps you see how society shapes behavior — and how behavior shapes society.

👥 Part 1: Social Groups

Groups shape identity, belonging, loyalty, pressure, and behavior. Click the tabs below to compare types of groups.

👨‍👩‍👧

Primary Group

A small, close-knit group where relationships are personal, emotionally meaningful, and long-lasting. Examples include family, close friends, or a trusted support circle.

🏢

Secondary Group

A larger, more formal, goal-oriented group where relationships are often temporary or task-focused. Examples include classmates, coworkers, teams, or committees.

🤝

In-Group

A group a person identifies with and feels loyalty or belonging toward. In-groups can create connection, pride, and support.

🚫

Out-Group

A group viewed as different from one’s own group. Out-groups may be treated with distance, competition, suspicion, or exclusion.

Sociology reminder: In-groups and out-groups are not just about personal feelings. They can also shape prejudice, stereotypes, favoritism, and social inequality.

📌

Reference Group

A group people use to evaluate themselves, their choices, or their behavior — even if they are not members of that group.

Example A student may compare their college plans to classmates, influencers, siblings, or people in their desired career field.

⚖️

Dominant Group

A group with greater access to power, status, and resources. Dominant groups often influence cultural norms and institutional expectations.

🧭

Minoritized Group

A group with less social power or access to resources that may experience discrimination, exclusion, or inequality.

⚠️ Part 2: Deviance & Social Control

Deviance is not always about “bad” behavior. Sociologists ask: Who made the rule? Who broke it? Who gets punished? Who gets excused?

⚠️ Deviance

Behavior or actions that violate social norms and provoke social reaction or disapproval.

✔️ Conformity

Adjusting behavior or beliefs to align with group expectations and norms.

🛑 Social Control

The ways societies encourage conformity and discourage deviance through laws, rewards, punishments, and social pressure.

👮 Obedience

Following instructions or commands from a person in a position of authority.

🏷️ Part 3: Theories of Deviance

These theories explain deviance from different angles. Click each question to reveal the theory connection.

Labeling Theory: Labels placed on people can shape identity and encourage continued deviant behavior. The reaction to behavior can become part of the problem.
Strain Theory: Deviance may occur when people cannot achieve cultural goals through socially accepted means.
Differential Association Theory: Deviant behavior is learned through close relationships and social interaction.

🧠 Part 4: Group Influence

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Peer Influence

The impact peers have on attitudes, values, behaviors, and decision-making.

🧠 Groupthink

A group decision-making pattern where harmony becomes more important than critical thinking or evaluating risks.

📖 Norms

Shared rules and expectations that guide behavior within groups and societies.

🧭 Leadership Styles

Different ways leaders influence groups, including authoritarian, democratic, and laissez-faire leadership.

Quick Check: Groupthink

A student club agrees to a risky plan because nobody wants to “cause drama” by disagreeing. What concept best fits?

🎭 Part 5: Dramaturgy & Social Performance

Dramaturgy compares social life to a stage performance. We often adjust behavior depending on the audience, setting, role, and expectations.

1

🏅 Status

A social position a person occupies within a group or society.

2

🎬 Role

The expected behaviors, obligations, and privileges attached to a particular status.

3

📜 Scripts

Guidelines or expectations that shape behavior in specific social situations.

4

👔 Costumes & Props

Clothing, appearance, and objects people use to support social performances.

🌟 Front Stage

Public settings where people perform expected social roles, such as work, school, interviews, or social media.

🏠 Back Stage

Private settings where people can relax, prepare, vent, or step away from public performances.

A person may act professional and polished during a job interview, then go home and relax in sweatpants while talking casually with family. Same person — different stage, audience, and performance.
