Access throughout the book

Accessibility, accommodations, practice activities, and privacy

Accessibility commitment. This coursebook was designed and reviewed using WCAG 2.1 Level AA and ADA Title II accessibility principles. It uses semantic headings, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, alternative text, labeled controls, readable type, responsive layouts, and reduced-motion support. Accessibility is an ongoing process; no automated or editorial review can guarantee access for every person, device, or assistive technology.

Disability access and accommodations. If a page, image, chart, video, download, or activity creates a barrier, contact the instructor and the college Disability Services/Accessibility Office using the current syllabus or college website. An accessible alternative or reasonable accommodation will be provided as promptly as possible and without penalty.

Practice—not grades. Interactives in this book are ungraded practice unless Canvas explicitly says otherwise. Core ideas also appear in text, captions, tables, explanations, and feedback. Students may skip an interaction or request an equivalent text-based activity without a grade penalty.

Privacy. The coursebook does not ask students to create an account or submit identifying information. Practice responses remain on the device unless a linked tool clearly states otherwise. Submit graded work only through Canvas or another institutionally approved system.

Career connections across SOC-210

Sociology travels into nearly every workplace

People and communities

Social work, counseling, education, healthcare, community outreach, and nonprofit leadership use culture, socialization, inequality, and institutions to understand needs without blaming individuals.

Organizations and business

Human resources, management, user-experience research, marketing, and organizational development apply groups, roles, networks, culture, leadership, and demographic change.

Law, policy, and public service

Criminal justice, government, law, public administration, and policy analysis use research, ethics, social control, stratification, and institutional analysis.

Data, media, and technology

Researchers, analysts, journalists, designers, and trust-and-safety specialists study evidence, social construction, algorithms, digital communities, cultural lag, and unequal access.

Transferable skill: Sociology teaches students to move from an individual story to a social pattern, test assumptions with evidence, recognize unequal consequences, and communicate with people whose experiences differ from their own.

Appendix A: Key concepts

Concept Working definition
Sociology The scientific study of society, social relationships, and patterned behavior.
Sociological imagination The ability to connect personal experience with social structure and historical conditions.
Social construction of reality The process through which people and institutions create, teach, and reinforce shared meanings.
Thomas theorem When people define a situation as real, their definition can produce real consequences.
Social structure Enduring patterns of relationships, roles, institutions, and unequal access to resources.
Social fact A collective pattern, rule, or institution that exists beyond one person and shapes behavior.

Appendix B: Using APA style

APA style helps readers identify where information came from. In SOC‑210, cite ideas, facts, data, images, and wording that originated elsewhere. Follow the specific assignment directions and use APA 7th edition.

Paraphrase with citation

Pattern: Explain the idea in your own sentence, then cite the author and year.

Example: Sociological imagination links personal experience to social history (Mills, 1959).
